# Glyph Codex

The brief for creators who explain GlyphCity's glyphs. The app names each glyph
and never explains it. You explain it. This codex keeps the parts that must
never change fixed, and leaves the rest to you.

Source of truth: `packages/shared/constants/archetype-catalog.ts` (names),
`packages/shared/constants/archetype-icons.ts` (icons),
`packages/shared/constants/genre-weights.ts` (how music maps to a glyph).
Spec: `.kiro/specs/glyphcity-rebrand/` Requirement 10.4.

## Two layers

| Layer                                                 | Who owns it           | Can you riff on it?                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Glyph names and icons                                 | GlyphCity             | No. Say and spell them exactly as written. |
| Colour means venue category                           | GlyphCity             | No. It is a map signal.                    |
| Beam height and brightness mean live presence         | GlyphCity             | No. It is the honesty promise.             |
| What each glyph is like, jokes, scenarios, characters | You and your comments | Yes, freely.                               |

## The canon

1. **A glyph is a music taste.** The app works it out from the music a person
   connects or picks. It never comes from race, language, neighbourhood,
   money, gender or age, and you never suggest it does.
2. **Fifteen glyphs, fixed names.** "The Firecracker", not "Firecrackers" or
   "the fire one". The names are how people search for you.
3. **Colour is the venue category**, the same six everywhere on the map:
   food, coffee, nightlife, retail, fitness, arts. A glyph's colour tells you
   what kind of place it is, never what kind of person is inside.
4. **The beam is the room right now.** Taller and brighter means more people
   are actually there. The app's words, in order: Be the first in, Quiet, A
   little busy, Busy, Very busy. Never say a beam is tall because a venue paid,
   and never stage a crowd for a video.
5. **Disclose every arrangement.** Paid posts, free entry, guest list and gets
   all count. Put #Ad or the platform's paid-partnership label up front, on
   every account you post from (ARB Code Appendix K).
6. **No drinking on camera**, and no one under 18 in nightlife content.

are starting points for your content, not rules for real people.

### The Festival Spirit

- Is: first to the stage, knows every chorus, turns strangers into a crew.
- Is not: a tourist at the party, quiet in the corner, there for the photo.

### The Conscious Creative

- Is: notes app full of ideas, finds the meaning in the lyric, makes the flyer.
- Is not: preachy, closed to new sounds, in it for status.

### The Township Royal

- Is: proud of where the sound comes from, dressed for the occasion, knows the DJ by name.
- Is not: a joke about money or a place, a costume, someone else's story to tell.
- Introduced only by township-rooted creators, in their own words and
  languages, and tested with that audience before any paid push (R10.5).

### The Sacred Rebel

- Is: Sunday morning and Saturday night, sings the hook like a hymn, holds their own line.
- Is not: mocking faith, mocking the party, a contradiction to be laughed at.

### The Firecracker

- Is: front of the room when the drop lands, loudest cheer, last to sit down.
- Is not: a fight starter, careless with people around them, a hazard.

### The Heritage Groover

- Is: knows the moves their parents knew, teaches the step, keeps the circle going.
- Is not: stuck in the past, a museum piece, against anything new.

### The Midnight Philosopher

- Is: deep talk at the bar, picks the corner table, notices the bassline.
- Is not: gloomy, superior, bored by the dancefloor.

### The Street Poet

- Is: catches the line nobody else heard, freestyles on the walk home, tells it straight.
- Is not: angry for show, a caricature of the street, only about tough talk.

### The Soul Wanderer

- Is: three venues in one night, follows a feeling, finds the room with the live band.
- Is not: lost, flaky, unable to commit to a plan.

### The Vibe Architect

- Is: clocks the lighting, rates the sound system, plans the night out in order.
- Is not: a snob, someone who cannot enjoy an imperfect room.

### The Smooth Operator

- Is: arrives at the right time, knows the bartender, makes it look easy.
- Is not: a player, cold, fake.

### The Groove Seeker

- Is: will travel for the right set, first one dancing, last one out.
- Is not: picky about the genre, standing still.

### The Culture Curator

- Is: knows the history behind the track, plays elders' records, introduces the classics.
- Is not: a gatekeeper, dismissive of young sounds.

### The Eclectic

- Is: playlist that makes no sense and works, friends from every scene, open to anything.
- Is not: "no taste", indecisive, a fallback.

### The Uncharted

- Is: new here, about to find out.
- Is not: boring or empty. It is the invitation to connect music and see what you are.

## Formats that work

- "Which glyph are you": reveal your own glyph from the app, then react.
- "The fifteen glyphs as...": taxi passengers, at 2am, at a braai, in a group chat.
- "Read the map": stand outside a venue, show its glyph and beam, then show the
  room. The beam must match the room.
- "Post your glyph": duet or stitch with a share card.

## Words to use and avoid

- Use the app's busy words when describing a beam: Quiet, A little busy, Busy,
  Very busy, Be the first in.
- Say "glyph" for the symbol. "Glyph" alone is never the name of the app in
  the same sentence; say GlyphCity.
- Never pair a glyph with a race, ethnic group, language group or income level,
  even as a joke.
